Overview

KU-THIA shares academic essays on international affairs with students. This post introduces an essay on how the European Union began and grew, from Robert Schuman's 1950 proposal for a coal and steel community to today's union of 27 countries and 24 official languages.

I created and designed the content: picking the key milestones and designing the carousel. The caption opens with the line “Because our differences are what help us learn from each other,” which ties the EU's story to why learning about other countries matters.

MessageCopyLayoutTeam reviewPublishing
View the post on Instagram

Problem Statement

The European Union's story spans more than 70 years of treaties, and academic essays take time to read. Students interested in international affairs want to learn it, but a dense essay is easy to skip in a fast-scrolling feed. The club needed a post that makes the topic inviting and easy to follow, one swipe at a time.

Goals

Clear at a glance

One key message from the essay, readable on a phone without zooming.

Made for students who follow world affairs

Visuals and wording for students interested in international affairs, friendly rather than textbook-like.

Get people reading

A clear call to action that leads straight to the full essay.

Content Process

How the post went from essay to feed.

  1. Read the essayPull out the key milestones: 1950, 1957, 1993 and today's 27 member countries.
  2. Define the audienceStudents interested in international affairs who want the story told clearly.
  3. Design in CanvaAn 8-slide carousel in EU blue and gold, sized for a phone screen.
  4. Review with the PR teamCheck facts, tone and visuals before posting.
  5. Publish and engagePosted on Instagram on 1 October 2026 with #kuthia and #kuinternationalaffairs.

What I Learned

  • Start from the one sentence the reader should remember, then design around it.

  • The phone screen is the canvas. Big type and one strong visual beat a crowded layout.

  • Planning with the PR team keeps every post on-message and on schedule.

The Mission

Workshop mission

Drawing Character

  1. Attach a pen to the AGV robot car.
  2. Steer it from a tablet to draw the character on paper.

What It Taught Me

  • Know your audience. Kids and parents need different messages, just like different customer groups.

  • Keep it simple. If a kid gets the mission in a minute, the message is clear enough.

  • Make it hands-on. People stay engaged when they can take part, not just watch.
